# Terminology and fact preservation

## Authority order

1. Official person, company, production, exchange, or distributor page
2. Official credits, filings, press releases, and licensed source material
3. Established locale-specific reference used only to resolve spelling

Do not use search-result snippets as final authority. Record the chosen official form in the article's verification pairs.

## Translation rules

- Preserve a person's identity and established stage name. Do not invent transliterations.
- Preserve official work titles where a released localized title exists; otherwise retain the original title and add a restrained transliteration only when useful.
- Preserve ticker symbols, exchange codes, corporate suffixes, model names, and branded capitalization.
- Preserve every numeric value and its meaning. Locale-format separators, dates, currencies, and units only after checking that the underlying value and time zone remain unchanged.
- Preserve uncertainty: `reported`, `estimated`, `expected`, and `alleged` must not become facts.
- Translate quotations only from an authorized full source. Mark paraphrases as paraphrases and never fabricate direct speech.
- Keep relationships, chronology, credits, and attribution intact.

## Search phrasing

- Write a natural locale-native title and description around the reader's actual question.
- Keep one primary intent per page. Do not repeat actor names, tickers, or work titles solely for ranking.
- Avoid literal Korean sentence order in English and Japanese. Favor fluent target-language syntax without changing claims.

## Per-article glossary

Track each sensitive term as a verification pair with a stable `id`, exact source form, chosen localized form, and `verified: true`. Recheck the glossary when the source hash changes.
