Dwayne Johnson Takes Maui from Voice Performance to Live Action in Moana

A source-based look at Dwayne Johnson's live-action Maui, his producing role, and the film's July 26 Korean box-office result.

SS.Desk Editorial TeamJuly 27, 2026
SS.Desk GlobalDwayne Johnson

Moana

Still No. 3 in Korea on its nineteenth day

Disney's live-action Moana opened in South Korea on July 8, 2026. On July 26, its nineteenth day in local cinemas, it ranked No. 3 on the Korean daily box-office chart and drew 34,802 admissions. Those Korean Film Council figures are cited by the original Korean article and describe one day rather than the film's eventual total. Dwayne Johnson's connection to the release is broader than a leading performance: he plays Maui and also participated as a producer.

In an official Disney interview published on July 9, Johnson discussed returning to Maui and recording the new song ‘Along the Way.’ His relationship with the character began in animation, where voice, timing and singing did most of the expressive work. The live-action production adds physical performance, practical staging and his involvement behind the camera. That continuity makes this more than a case of an actor being recast in a familiar property. The same performer is adapting a role he has already developed in two animated films.

Turning an animated demigod into a physical performance

Maui is a demigod associated with transformation and a powerful fishhook. Johnson voiced him in the 2016 animated Moana and again in Moana 2 in 2024. In the live-action film, he has to place the character in front of the camera with his full body. Maui's strength and confidence are obvious features, but the character also argues, jokes and gradually acts as Moana's companion on the voyage. The performance therefore depends on rhythm and relationship, not only on physical size.

The useful comparison is not whether every animated gesture has been copied. Voice acting can concentrate expression into a line, a breath or a song; live action also asks what the eyes, posture and movement communicate between words. Johnson has years of familiarity with Maui's comic tempo, but the new format changes the scale of every choice. His producing role adds another dimension because he was involved in the project beyond the time spent on set. The central question is how he preserves the warmth and humor audiences recognize while giving the live-action version its own texture rather than treating it as a mechanical reconstruction.

A career built across wrestling, film and production

Johnson was born on May 2, 1972, and was 54 on July 27, 2026. He made his WWE debut at Survivor Series in 1996 and began his film career with The Mummy Returns in 2001. Professional wrestling trained him to project a clear character, control physical movement and respond to an audience in real time. Those skills transferred naturally to action and comedy, where a scene can depend as much on timing and presence as on dialogue. WWE's official profile records eight WWE Championship reigns.

His screen credits cited by the Korean source include The Scorpion King, the Fast & Furious films, the Jumanji films, Jungle Cruise and Black Adam. He also established Seven Bucks Productions and developed work for film and television. In 2026, The Walt Disney Company named him among that year's Disney Legends. Moana gathers those strands in one place: Johnson is an actor, singer and producer revisiting a character first created through voice work. The July 26 Korean ranking shows that the film remained visible well beyond opening day, while the performance itself offers a clearer measure of his current career—familiar star presence applied to a role he has helped carry across different forms.
