Matt Damon Carries the Weight of Homecoming as Odysseus in The Odyssey

Matt Damon stars as Odysseus in The Odyssey, which ranked No. 1 in North America on July 27 and became the first feature shot entirely with IMAX film cameras.

SS.Desk Editorial TeamJuly 29, 2026
SS.Desk GlobalMatt Damon

The Odyssey

No. 1 in North America on July 27

Matt Damon stars as Odysseus in Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey. The film opened on July 17, 2026, and, according to the Box Office Mojo figure cited by the original Korean article, ranked No. 1 at the North American daily box office on July 27. Its gross for that day was $14,717,905. Universal announced on July 20 that The Odyssey debuted as the No. 1 film worldwide, earning $263.8 million in global ticket sales. These are dated measurements rather than forecasts: they show where the film stood early in its theatrical run, not where its final domestic or worldwide total would end.

Damon took part in the official promotion around the release. In NBCUniversal material about the production and its IMAX 70mm presentation, he discussed the location work and the scale of filming across different countries. The official footage shows practical environments, large groups of soldiers and the physical presence of the Trojan Horse. The campaign therefore gives viewers two ways into the film: the technical ambition of the production and the human purpose of a warrior who is trying to return home.

Odysseus and the direction of home

Odysseus begins his long voyage toward Ithaca after the Trojan War. The new official trailer referenced by the source includes Damon's Odysseus meeting Calypso, played by Charlize Theron. He is seen as a leader in battle, but the character is not defined by command alone. Islands, open water and repeated dangers place pressure on the one direction he is trying to preserve: home. The return is both a physical route and the motive that keeps the character moving after war.

NBCUniversal described The Odyssey as the first feature shot entirely with IMAX film cameras. Official behind-the-scenes material also emphasizes real locations, large-scale staging and physical production elements. Those choices can make the voyage feel vast, yet the acting challenge remains intimate. Damon has to express accumulated fatigue, tactical responsibility and the determination to continue without allowing one quality to erase the others. The most useful performance question is not simply whether Odysseus looks heroic in battle. It is whether the audience can feel the cost of every stage of the journey and still understand why he refuses to abandon it.

An actor who also writes and produces

Damon was born on October 8, 1970, and was 55 on July 29, 2026. He made an early screen appearance in Mystic Pizza in 1988. His 1997 breakthrough was tied to authorship as well as performance: he co-wrote Good Will Hunting with Ben Affleck and starred in it. They won the Academy Award for the screenplay, while Damon was also nominated for Best Actor. That combination established a career in which acting, writing and producing could support one another rather than remain separate tracks.

His later credits span war drama, crime entertainment, action franchises, science fiction and historical biography. The Korean source highlights Saving Private Ryan, the Ocean's films, the Bourne series, The Martian, Ford v Ferrari and Oppenheimer. The list matters less as a catalogue than as evidence of range: Damon has repeatedly moved between outwardly physical roles and characters whose main conflict is internal. Odysseus asks for both. The large-format photography and practical scale surround the character, but the emotional line still depends on a tired man maintaining his intention to return. Damon's long experience across performance and production makes that balance the clearest reason to watch his work here.
